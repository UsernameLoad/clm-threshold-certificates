\begin{abstract}
We report a certified numerical bench for the two-dimensional inviscid Boussinesq equations in the Hou--Luo boundary-blowup configuration --- a periodic strip with no-flow walls and the Luo--Hou driver data --- when the temperature equation carries a fractional thermal diffusion $-\kappa(-\Delta)^{\sigma/2}\theta$ with no-flux walls and $\sigma\in[0,2]$ a dial. The level is certified by a registered battery of machine-floor anchors at every $\sigma$, including the degenerate $\sigma=0$ operator. On it we record four things. First, on the saturated branch the certified driver-collapse observables depend on $(\kappa,\sigma)$ only through $X=\kappa\kd^\sigma$, $\kd$ the driver wavenumber, so that the response law $\Omega_\infty\kappa=66.3\,\kd^{2-\sigma}$ holds down the dial, confirmed at $\sigma=0.5$ to $0.1\%$ over a twofold range of $\kappa$. Second, the structure of the solution does not collapse: at matched physical time and matched physical band the $x$-spectra of the vorticity and of the wall temperature are ordered strictly monotonically in $\sigma$ --- the larger $\sigma$, the steeper the spectrum --- at every mode above the amplitude floor and at every certified time, the vorticity ordering being converged between $512^2$ and $1024^2$ grids to $0.15\%$ mode by mode; its magnitude grows backward in time on a fixed set of modes, and at a fixed time it decreases weakly with the driver damping. Third, a ``valley'' in a fitted analyticity-strip parameter reported in earlier drafts of this program was a fit-degeneracy artifact and is withdrawn; we record the lesson as a methods section. Fourth, the wall-temperature spectrum at $512^2$ is over-steepened for $\sigma\ge1.375$ by a resolution effect largest at the wall row and decaying through the temperature's content layer, whose anisotropic signature we measure: it is carried mostly by the along-wall resolution, least by the wall-normal resolution of the layer, and in part by both together. The vorticity spectrum, whose layer is grid-converged in each direction separately, is the clean carrier. No analyticity-strip width and no functional form for the $\sigma$-separation's time or $X$ dependence are claimed.
\end{abstract}

\section{Configuration and the \texorpdfstring{$\sigma$}{sigma}-dial level}\label{sec:config}

\paragraph{The bench.} The 2D incompressible Boussinesq equations on the periodic-in-$x$ strip, $x\in[0,L_x)$ with $L_x=1/6$ (the Luo--Hou $z$-period \cite{LH14}), $y\in[0,1]$ between two walls:
\begin{align*}
&\omega_t+u\cdot\nabla\omega=\theta_x,\qquad \theta_t+u\cdot\nabla\theta=-\kappa(-\Delta)^{\sigma/2}\theta,\\
&u=(-\psi_y,\psi_x),\qquad \Delta\psi=\omega,\qquad \psi=0\ \text{at }y=0,1\ \text{(no-flow walls)},
\end{align*}
with no-flux walls for $\theta$, $\omega$ inviscid, and the exact Hou--Luo driver data \cite{LH14}
\[
\theta^0=A\,e^{-60(y(2-y))^4}\sin^2(2\pi x/L_x),\qquad \omega^0=0,\qquad A=10^4;
\]
the symmetry class is $\omega$ odd and $\theta$ even in $x$. The corner is $(x,y)=(0,0)$; the driver wavenumber is $\kd=4\pi/L_x=24\pi=75.398$. Discretisation: Fourier in $x$, Chebyshev--Gauss--Lobatto in $y$ ($N+1$ points), $2/3$ dealiasing in both directions, fourth-order Runge--Kutta with an adaptive CFL step ($\mathrm{cfl}=0.4$ throughout), the Poisson solve by a Chebyshev-tau quasi-inverse certified against a dense-LU reference. The base solver's anchor battery (exact solutions, invariants, manufactured solutions, resolution floors) is our earlier record and is not repeated here.

\paragraph{The certified window and the verdicts.} Every run carries its own certification: a diagnostic record is \emph{certified} while the spectral tails in $x$ and $y$ are both $\le10^{-6}$ of the field; $\tcert$ is the last certified time, $\Om$ the certified peak of $\sup\abs\omega$, $g_{\rm cert}$ the certified growth factor, $\mathrm{itx}=\int\sup\abs{\theta_x}\,dt$ the driver impulse. Verdicts at the stop: SATURATED (the driver's impulse and $\sup\abs\omega$ both stop growing to within $2\%$ over the trailing $25\%$ window --- the $\kappa$-world's analogue of decay, since $\omega$ has no dissipation channel), RESOLUTION (the tails pass $10^{-2}$ --- the front has reached the grid while still growing), BLOWUP-bar and UNDET($T_{\max}$) (never fired on this program). Only certified quantities enter any statement below; the uncertified endgame after $\tcert$ is used for one explicitly labelled observation (Section~\ref{sec:layer}). Throughout, a \emph{registered letter} is an outcome label whose deciding statistic and bar were fixed before the comparison was run; we report every letter as it fired.

\paragraph{The $\sigma$-dial level.} The fractional operator is defined spectrally on the strip: with the two Neumann rows eliminated, the reduced $y$-operator is $A_{\rm red}(k_x)=A_0+k_x^2I$ --- the eigenvectors are $k_x$-independent, $A_0$'s spectrum is real and non-negative ($\max\abs{\operatorname{Im}\lambda}=0$ exactly), it matches the Neumann eigenvalues $(n\pi)^2$ to $2.5\cdot10^{-13}$ / $3.3\cdot10^{-13}$ / $1.5\cdot10^{-12}$ at $N_y=32$ / $64$ / $128$, and the eigenbasis is well conditioned ($\operatorname{cond}W=2.66$ / $3.36$ / $4.18$). The diffusive half-step of the Strang split is therefore the exact exponential $\theta\leftarrow W\operatorname{diag}(\exp(-\kappa(\lambda_n+k_x^2)^{\sigma/2}h))W^{-1}\theta$, unconditionally stable at every $\sigma$ including $\sigma=0$. Convention, stated because it is a choice: $0^{\sigma/2}:=0$ for every $\sigma\ge0$, so constants are annihilated at every $\sigma$ and the $\sigma=0$ operator is $I-P_0$. $\sigma=2$ is the ordinary Laplacian; in the $\abs D^\beta$ convention of the well-posedness literature our $\sigma$ is $\beta$ (Section~\ref{sec:lit}).

\paragraph{Certification (the registered battery).} All items met at their predicted floors, three after an honest test redesign recorded in the run log: (a) the eigen-decomposition as above; (b) pure-eigenmode decay against $\exp(-\kappa\lambda^{\sigma/2}t)$ at $\sigma\in\{0,0.5,1,1.5,2\}$: $4.57\cdot10^{-14}$ worst case; (c) the $\sigma=2$ level against the certified Crank--Nicolson $\kappa$-level: $dt$-halving ratios $3.99$ / $4.00$ / $4.00$; (d) $\kappa=0$ bit-identical to the inviscid solver ($0.0$); (e) the $\sigma=0$ degenerate operator on random compatible data $1.14\cdot10^{-14}$ / $7.25\cdot10^{-15}$ with the operator identity $\abs{D(h)v-(P_0+e^{-\kappa h}(I-P_0))v}=2.49\cdot10^{-14}$; (f) no-flux enforcement from wall-incompatible data $7.54\cdot10^{-15}$; (h) the eigenvalue error at $n=N_y/3$ falling $3.06\cdot10^{-9}\to6.9\cdot10^{-15}\to6.3\cdot10^{-16}$ under doubling. One registered item was REFUTED and converted: (g), exact conservation of the $\theta$-mean, fails at the $3\cdot10^{-11}$ ($\sigma=0$) to $1.2\cdot10^{-8}$ ($\sigma=2$) level on compatible data because the strip-integral functional is not a left null vector of $A_0$; the mean drift is carried in every run as a free resolution monitor, and it is $\sigma$-restricted: under a grid doubling it falls at $\sigma=2$ and rises at $\sigma=0.5$ (the weakly damped spurious modes), so the monitor is read only at $\sigma\ge1$.

\section{The saturated-branch collapse and the \texorpdfstring{$\sigma$}{sigma}-extended saturation law}\label{sec:law}

Registered before the runs at $\sigma=0.5$ (predicted $66.3\,\kd^{1.5}=4.3407\cdot10^4$, bar $3\%$), the measured plateaus are $\Om\cdot\kappa=4.33869\cdot10^4$ / $4.34170\cdot10^4$ / $4.34478\cdot10^4$ at $\kappa=392.8194$ / $261.8796$ / $196.4097$ --- deviations $-0.045\%$ / $+0.024\%$ / $+0.095\%$, mutually consistent to $0.140\%$ over a $2.00\times$ range of $\kappa$; the $\sigma=2$ reproduction obligation was discharged first ($66.354$ / $66.416$ / $66.393$ at $\kappa=0.60$ / $0.40$ / $0.30$, i.e.\ $+0.08$ / $+0.18$ / $+0.14\%$ against $66.3$). \textbf{Consequence, stated exactly:} the inverse-first-power form holds at a $\sigma$ where it had never been tested, so on the accessible saturated branch there is no finite-$\kappa$ divergence at $\sigma=0.5$ either; $\kappa_c(\sigma)=0$ down the dial is an extrapolation of a confirmed law, never a measurement, and it is labelled so wherever it appears.

\paragraph{The object.} For a row $(\kappa,\sigma)$ and a field $f$, let $\mathrm{amp}_f(k)$ be the amplitude of the $x$-Fourier mode $k$: for $\theta$ the modulus of the transform of the driver-wall row (the wall row carries the $\theta$ structure, Section~\ref{sec:layer}), for $\omega$ the maximum over $y$ of $\abs{\mathcal F_x\omega}$ (the solver's own strip object). \section{Methods: what the earlier drafts got wrong, and the rules that replaced it}\label{sec:methods}

\paragraph{The withdrawn ``valley''.} Earlier drafts of this program read a two-shape fit $\ln\mathrm{amp}=a+p\ln k-\delta k$ on the $\theta$ wall's fit band $[\kd,0.2N]$ at each run's own stop and reported $\delta$'s interior minimum in $\sigma$ as a structure (``the analyticity strip is narrowest at $\sigma\approx1.5$ / $1.375$ / $1.25$ / $1.125\dots$'', moving with damping and with refinement). An audit of the banked records found: the fit's prefactor and exponential slots are correlated at $+0.90$--$0.93$ (a property of the basis on that band, independent of the field --- $\operatorname{corr}(p,\delta)=+0.908$ at every $512^2$ row); the one-grid-step margins carrying every position statement were $0.2$--$1.6$ of the fit's own formal $\sigma_\delta$; the minimum moves with the fit band; under every one-shape variant ($p$ fixed at the column mean, $p$ fixed at $-4$, the pure exponential on the upper half / upper quarter / full band) every resolution-class column is monotone increasing in $\sigma$ with no interior minimum; the solver's own $\omega$ strip series, read at the matched time, is monotone at every column; and at the fourth $X$ the ``minimum'' sat exactly where the stop criterion switched from SATURATED to RESOLUTION. The rebuild then showed, with the stop-time confound removed, that the two-shape $\delta$-slot still puts its minima at the old positions at matched time ($\theta$ at $X_2$: $0.08981$ / $0.08963$ / $0.08880$ / $0.08660$ / $0.08522$ / $0.08595$ / $0.09031$ across $0.875\to1.5$, $\sigma_\delta$ $0.0018$--$0.0025$, a minimum at $1.25$ with $1.375$ within $0.3\,\sigma_\delta$; at $X_3$ a minimum at $1.125$) while the model-free object and every one-shape fit are monotone: \textbf{the valley was the degenerate $(p,\delta)$ pair's crossing point --- a property of the fit, not of the field or the stop time --- and every position statement of the earlier drafts is withdrawn.} The lesson is recorded as a rule: a fitted structure parameter enters a statement only with its formal uncertainty printed, a model-robustness check (nested and alternative models, sub-band convergence) and matched stop criteria and times across the compared rows.

\paragraph{Matched band and matched time.} Two ``$\times2$ between $N=512$ and $N=1024$'' statements of an earlier record were comparison artifacts: the tail-fit band scaled with $N$ ($52..102$ at $512^2$ but $104..204$ at $1024^2$; on the same physical modes the ratio is $1.07$--$1.19$), and the strip slopes were read at each column's own $\tcert$ (at a common time the ratio is $1.047$--$1.053$). The local slope on a band centred at $k_c$ decomposes as $\delta_{V0}+\abs p/k_c$ with the fitted prefactor $p\approx-3.5\dots-4.5$ on the $\theta$ wall, so every ``tail $\delta$'' on the record is dominated by $\abs p/k_c$ and the asymptotic exponential regime $k\gg\abs p/\delta$ ($\approx3000$ at $1024^2$) lies beyond every $N$ we ran: \textbf{no analyticity-strip width has been measured on this bench; what the instruments measure is a local log-slope on a named band at a named time.} Rule: cross-$N$ and cross-column comparisons are made at matched physical band and matched time --- cross-column at one physical time --- on a model-free object where one exists.

\paragraph{Fixed mode sets across a course, and letters at the instrument's resolution.} A band mean taken over a mask that grows in time (the floor mask opening from half of $\Bst$ at a window's start) rises before the physics is seen, because $\abs{S(k)}$ grows with $k$; five of eight registered time-course magnitude letters in an earlier pass fired on that mask edge (recorded as fired; the labelled diagnostic on a fixed set beside them). Rule: a time-course or cross-time letter on a band-mean object holds its mode set fixed across the course; a cross-column or cross-$N$ comparison is made on one fixed set (its size printed as the validity condition) and, for the $N$-comparison, on one window. \section{Where this sits in the literature}\label{sec:lit}

Statements below are cited for what the sources state, with their theorem numbers; a source not held at full text is cited at metadata level and marked ``not read''.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item \textbf{The dial and its critical exponent.} Hmidi, Keraani and Rousset \cite[Theorem~1.1]{HKR11} prove for the Euler--Boussinesq system on $\R^2$ with the critical thermal diffusion $\abs D\theta$ (their (1.2): $\partial_tv+v\cdot\nabla v+\nabla p=\theta e_2$, $\partial_t\theta+v\cdot\nabla\theta+\abs D\theta=0$, $\operatorname{div}v=0$) that for $p\in\,]2,\infty[$, $v_0\in B^1_{\infty,1}\cap W^{1,p}$ divergence-free and $\theta_0\in B^0_{\infty,1}\cap L^p$ ``there exists a unique global solution $(v,\theta)$ to the system (1.2)''. Their introduction records that Hmidi and Zerguine \cite{HZ10} (not read; the statement as cited in \cite[\S1]{HKR11} and in \cite[\S1]{SWXY26}) ``proved the global well-posedness for fractional diffusion $D_\theta=-\abs D^\alpha$ for $\alpha\in\,]1,2[$'', the case $\alpha=1$ being what \cite{HKR11} adds; their companion paper \cite{HKR10} treats velocity dissipation with an undiffused temperature, a different configuration from ours (\cite[\S1]{SWXY26} cites the same result as ``$\alpha=0$, $\beta\in(1,2]$''). In the $\abs D^\beta$ convention our $\sigma$ is $\beta$: on $\R^2$ the dial is covered at $\sigma=1$ by \cite{HKR11}, for $1<\sigma<2$ by \cite{HZ10} as cited, and at $\sigma=2$ --- the Laplacian with inviscid velocity --- by Chae \cite{Chae06} and Hou and Li \cite{HL05}, which \cite[\S1]{SWXY26} records as ``the global regularity to (1.1) with $\alpha=2$, $\beta=0$ or $\alpha=0$, $\beta=2$''; $\sigma<1$ is not.
\item \textbf{The regime map.} Stefanov, Wu, Xu and Ye \cite[\S1]{SWXY26} state, for the 2D Boussinesq equations on $\R^2$ with $(-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}u$ and $(-\Delta)^{\beta/2}\theta$, the classification ``the subcritical regime ($\alpha+\beta>1$), the critical regime ($\alpha+\beta=1$) and the supercritical regime ($\alpha+\beta<1$)'' and that ``the global regularity problem for the supercritical regime remains largely out of reach, while substantial progress has been made in the critical and subcritical cases''; their Theorem~1.1 (global regularity in the subcritical regime) assumes $\alpha\in(0,1)$ and $\beta\in(0,1)$. Our configuration is $\alpha=0$ (inviscid velocity): the rows $\sigma<1$ of this bench are in the supercritical regime in their terms; the rows $\sigma\ge1$ are covered on $\R^2$ by \cite{HKR11,HZ10,Chae06,HL05}.
\item \textbf{Walls.} The bounded-domain result we hold is Zhao \cite[Theorem~1.1]{Zhao10}: for $\Omega\subset\R^2$ bounded and smooth, inviscid velocity with $U\cdot n|_{\partial\Omega}=0$ and $\theta_t+U\cdot\nabla\theta=\kappa\Delta\theta$ with the DIRICHLET condition $\theta|_{\partial\Omega}=\bar\theta$ (his compatibility conditions (1.4)), $H^3$ data give ``a unique solution $(U,\theta)$ to (1.1)--(1.2) globally in time'', $\theta$ converging exponentially to $\bar\theta$ with constants exponentially large in $1/\kappa$ (his Remark~1.1: $\nrm\omega_\infty\le\tilde c=O(\kappa^{-43}e^{21/\kappa})$). Our walls are no-flux for $\theta$, our diffusion is fractional, and our $\sigma\ge1$ rows sit at the critical exponent or below the Laplacian; the other boundary results we know of --- Lai, Pan and Zhao \cite{LPZ11} (not read; an initial-boundary-value problem for the viscous equations by its title) and Tan and Xu \cite{TX23} (not read; the half plane with partial viscosity by its title) --- and the whole-space partial-viscosity results \cite{Chae06,HL05} treat velocity viscosity or the Laplacian. \textbf{We know of no published well-posedness result covering fractional thermal diffusion with no-flux walls at any $\sigma$; the $\sigma<1$ rows are supercritical in the sense of \cite{SWXY26} and carry a boundary.} For the Laplacian case $\sigma=2$ with no-flux walls, Appendix~\ref{app:neumann} records a routine adaptation of \cite{Zhao10}; it is an adaptation argument, not a new theorem. The measured laws of Section~\ref{sec:law} ($\kappa_c(\sigma)=0$ as an extrapolation; the $1/\kappa$ response) are consistent with and adjacent to the known Dirichlet-$\theta$ theory; the response law is exponentially sharper than the $e^{21/\kappa}$-class bound the theorem provides. No theorem is claimed here.
\item \textbf{The corner.} The configuration is the 2D Boussinesq equations ``on the upper half space'' with the no-flow condition on the stream function, in which Chen and Hou \cite[Theorem~1]{CH22} prove that ``there is a family of smooth initial data $(\theta_0,\omega_0)$ with $\theta_0(x,y)$ even, $\omega_0(x,y)$ odd in $x$, $\theta_0(0,y)\equiv0$, and finite-energy velocity $u_0$ such that the solution of the 2D Boussinesq equations develops a singularity in finite time $T<+\infty$'', nearly self-similarly and stably in their symmetry class; our data are in that class on the strip, and our bench is the dissipative deformation of their setting. Chen, Huang and Li \cite[\S6]{CHL26} report numerically, in the same half-space setting, two-stage self-similar blowups with singular profiles from degenerate data. Both are inviscid; neither contains a dissipative statement, and nothing in this note bears on their theorems.
\item \textbf{Non-uniqueness.} Chen and Yin \cite[Theorem~2.1]{CY25} prove, for the Boussinesq system on the torus with $(-\Delta)^\alpha$ on both equations, $1\le\alpha<(d+1)/2$, sharp non-uniqueness of weak solutions in $L^p_tL^\infty_x$ for $p<2\alpha/(2\alpha-1)$. A different object (weak solutions; dissipation on both equations; no walls); recorded as context.
\item \textbf{Novelty grade.} The objects of Sections~\ref{sec:law}--\ref{sec:layer} are, to our knowledge, apparently uncomputed: a full-text census of the sources above (the terms fractional / dissipation / diffusion, critical / supercritical / subcritical, bounded domain / boundary / half / wall / Neumann / no-flux / Dirichlet, Hou / Luo / corner / hyperbolic, analytic / strip / spectrum / Fourier, saturation / threshold / blowup, numerics / simulation, every hit read in its sentence) found no statement about a dissipative response law at the Hou--Luo corner, a fractional-thermal-diffusion problem with walls, a $\sigma$-ordering of spectra under fractional dissipation, or a quantitative $\sigma$-dependence of a blowup-scenario observable; the configuration (fractional thermal diffusion at the Hou--Luo corner with walls) does not appear in the sources above. No ``first'' claim is made.

\section{The no-flux adaptation of Zhao's theorem at \texorpdfstring{$\sigma=2$}{sigma = 2}}\label{app:neumann}

This appendix records, at argument level, that the $\sigma=2$ configuration of this bench --- inviscid velocity, $\theta_t+U\cdot\nabla\theta=\kappa\Delta\theta$ with no-flux walls --- is covered by a routine adaptation of \cite[Theorem~1.1]{Zhao10}, whose boundary condition is Dirichlet. It settles for our own purposes the question ``is the no-flux configuration known-regular at $\sigma=2$'' at the level of a written adaptation; a submission-grade version would write out the five substituted estimates in full, which is mechanical and contains no new idea. It does not touch $\sigma<2$.

\begin{quote}
\textbf{Lemma.} Let $\Omega$ be either a smooth bounded domain in $\R^2$ or the periodic strip $\mathbb T_L\times[0,1]$. Consider 2D inviscid heat-conductive Boussinesq flow $U_t+U\cdot\nabla U+\nabla P=\theta e_2$, $\nabla\cdot U=0$, $\theta_t+U\cdot\nabla\theta=\kappa\Delta\theta$ ($\kappa>0$), with $U\cdot n=0$ and the no-flux condition $\partial\theta/\partial n=0$ on $\partial\Omega$ (data smooth, divergence-free, satisfying the Neumann-parabolic compatibility conditions). Then the solution is global and smooth; $\theta\to\bar\theta:=\abs\Omega^{-1}\int\theta_0$ exponentially in $H^3$; and $\nrm{\omega(\cdot,t)}_\infty\le\tilde c(\kappa)$ uniformly in $t$, with the same $\kappa$-dependence class as the Dirichlet case.
\end{quote}

\noindent\emph{Adaptation, step by step against \cite[\S3]{Zhao10}.}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
\item \emph{The conserved mean replaces the Dirichlet zero.} $\frac{d}{dt}\int\theta=\oint\kappa\,\partial\theta/\partial n-\oint(U\cdot n)\theta=0$ under exactly our two boundary conditions, so $\bar\theta$ is a constant of motion; set $\varphi:=\theta-\bar\theta$. Then $\varphi$ solves the same equation ($\bar\theta$ is constant in space and time) and the buoyancy sees only $\varphi$: $\theta_x\equiv\varphi_x$, and $\theta e_2=\varphi e_2+\bar\theta e_2$ with $\bar\theta e_2$ a gradient, absorbed into $P$. Every estimate below runs on $\varphi$.
\item \emph{Poincaré becomes Poincaré--Wirtinger (his (3.2)).} $\nrm\varphi\le c_0'\nrm{\nabla\varphi}$ for mean-zero $\varphi$, classical on bounded smooth $\Omega$ and on the strip. His Lemma~3.1 then gives $\nrm{\varphi(t)}^2\le\nrm{\varphi_0}^2e^{-2\beta_0t}$ verbatim: in (3.1) the transport term dies by $\nabla\cdot U=0$ with $\oint(U\cdot n)\theta^2=0$ (shared boundary condition), and the dissipation boundary term $\oint(\partial\theta/\partial n)\theta$ dies under no-flux exactly where Dirichlet killed it.
\item \emph{Dirichlet elliptic regularity becomes Neumann elliptic regularity (his Lemma~2.2, used at (3.14) and its $H^2$/$H^3$ repeats).} $\kappa\Delta\varphi=f:=\varphi_t+U\cdot\nabla\varphi$ with $\partial\varphi/\partial n=0$; the compatibility $\int f=\frac{d}{dt}\int\varphi+\int U\cdot\nabla\varphi=0$ holds automatically (step~1), and $\nrm\varphi_{W^{m+2,p}}\le(c_5'/\kappa)\nrm f_{W^{m,p}}$ for mean-zero $\varphi$ is the classical Neumann estimate (Agmon--Douglis--Nirenberg; Grisvard) on smooth $\Omega$; on the strip, Fourier in $x$ and the one-dimensional Neumann problem give it directly (no corners --- the strip case is the easier one).
\item \emph{Boundary-term symmetry at every differentiated level (his (3.41)--(3.42) and the Remark~3.1 repeats).} His only boundary inputs are $\{\theta|_\partial=0\Rightarrow\theta_t|_\partial=0$ on the fixed boundary$\}$ and $\{U\cdot n=0\Rightarrow U_t\cdot n=0\}$. The Neumann twins hold by the same time differentiation on the fixed boundary: $\partial\theta/\partial n=0\Rightarrow\partial\theta_t/\partial n=0$; $U_t\cdot n=0$ is shared. Every boundary integral in the $H^1$/$H^2$/$H^3$ chain (tested with $\varphi_t$, $\Delta\varphi$-type quantities, and the time-differentiated equation) dies under the Neumann twins exactly where the Dirichlet originals died. Checked term by term through his Lemmas~3.1--3.5: no estimate uses the sign or the trace value of $\theta$ on $\partial\Omega$, only that the boundary integrals vanish.
\item \emph{The velocity chain is untouched.} His Lemmas~2.3 (div-curl with $U\cdot n=0$), 2.5 (Euler local theory), 3.2 and 3.4 ((3.31)--(3.32): $\omega_t+U\cdot\nabla\omega=\varphi_x$ tested with $\abs\omega^{p-2}\omega$ --- no $\theta$-boundary contact) are velocity-side and identical; on the strip, Lemma~2.3 by Fourier in $x$. The weak-existence fixed point (his Lemma~2.6) runs on the Neumann heat semigroup with mean-zero spaces ($H^1\cap\{\text{mean }0\}$ replacing $H^1_0$) --- standard.
\end{enumerate}
Conclusion: Zhao's Theorem~3.1 chain closes with constants $c_0'$, $c_5'$ in place of $c_0$, $c_5$; the decay target is $\bar\theta$ instead of the boundary value; the $\kappa$-dependence classes (his $O(\kappa^{-1}e^{1/\kappa})$-type ladders) are unchanged in form. The compatibility-condition line in the data hypothesis is the standard Neumann-parabolic one, replacing his (1.4). Consequence for the claim language of Section~\ref{sec:lit}: the no-flux configuration at $\sigma=2$ is argued-regular, not mystery-open, and the measured $\kappa$-response law of Section~\ref{sec:law} is quantitative structure inside a regime that this argument covers, exponentially sharper than the provable $e^{21/\kappa}$-class vorticity bound.
